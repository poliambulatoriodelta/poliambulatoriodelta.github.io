BLSD: why training and refresher courses are essential to save a life

TD
Poliambulatorio Delta Team
· 26 August 2026 · 6 min read

A cardiac arrest can happen at any time and in any place. In the first few minutes, before the emergency services arrive, the chance of survival depends largely on how quickly the people next to the victim recognise the emergency, alert the emergency services, start cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and use an automated external defibrillator (AED), when one is available.

This is exactly where BLSD – Basic Life Support and Defibrillation training plays a central role: a course that provides the practical skills to respond to a cardiac arrest, using resuscitation manoeuvres and the defibrillator correctly.

BLSD training is not just a certificate

Attending a BLSD course means learning to turn an emergency, often marked by fear and intense stress, into a sequence of precise actions.

A trained rescuer must be able to quickly recognise a possible cardiac arrest, activate the emergency system, start high-quality CPR and use the AED following the device's instructions. The most recent ERC 2025 Guidelines on Basic Life Support update the recommendations on recognising cardiac arrest, chest compressions, rescue breaths and the use of the defibrillator.

Practical training is therefore essential: knowing a procedure in theory does not necessarily mean being able to apply it correctly when every second counts.

Regulatory updates in Italy

An important step was the Italian Ministry of Health Decree of 5 April 2025, published in the Official Gazette on 14 April 2025, which issued nationally valid guidelines for cardiopulmonary resuscitation and for certification in the use of the defibrillator (BLSD).

In January 2026 the Ministry of Health also published a communication dedicated to the guidelines for cardiopulmonary resuscitation and the use of the defibrillator in adults and children.

These updates are part of a broader regulatory process that in recent years has strengthened the spread of AEDs and the culture of emergency response. Italian law regulates the use of defibrillators and the training of authorised persons, within the framework of the national provisions in force.

It is important, however, to distinguish between updates to the scientific guidelines and the expiry of the authorisation/certification set by the specific training course: the duration and the retraining arrangements may depend on the training programme, the accredited body and the applicable legislation.

Why refresher training is necessary

BLSD skills are practical abilities. Like all abilities, they can fade over time if they are not practised. Refresher training makes it possible to:

  • review the response algorithm;
  • consolidate the quality of chest compressions;
  • stay familiar with the AED;
  • correct any errors picked up over time;
  • learn about the changes introduced by the new guidelines;
  • increase confidence and speed during an emergency.

The ERC 2025 Guidelines are today an up-to-date scientific reference for cardiopulmonary resuscitation and were developed on the basis of the international ILCOR evidence and recommendations.

Refresher training should therefore not be seen as a mere bureaucratic requirement. Above all, it is a tool for keeping truly operational the skills that might be needed in a life-or-death situation.

Training must be continuous

Effective training does not end with the first course. Regular practice of the manoeuvres, simulations and retraining help to maintain correct automatic responses and decision-making ability.

An organisation that hosts an AED should also promote a genuine emergency culture: knowing where the device is, checking its availability according to the established procedures and making sure that trained people know how to respond are essential elements of a truly effective system.

The Italian Resuscitation Council also stresses that the content and methods of training must be constantly updated in line with the latest scientific evidence and international guidelines.

From regulation to a culture of rescue

The availability of a defibrillator is not enough on its own. An AED must be quickly reachable and used within the established procedures, while those who respond must have the knowledge needed to recognise the emergency and start CPR promptly.

This is why BLSD training is an investment in the safety of the whole community: companies, schools, sports clubs, public and private facilities and, more generally, properly trained citizens can help create a faster and more effective rescue network.

Conclusions

The new national Guidelines issued in 2025 and the adoption of the ERC 2025 Guidelines confirm the importance of keeping training in cardiopulmonary resuscitation and in the use of the defibrillator up to date with the latest evidence.

BLSD should not be seen as just a course to attend in order to obtain a certificate. It is a concrete skill that requires practice, refresher training and awareness.

Training means being ready. Refreshing your training means being ready in the right way. In a cardiac arrest, this preparation can make the difference.

Note: this article is for information purposes. To check training obligations, the validity of a specific certification and retraining arrangements, please refer to the training course attended, the competent body and the legislation applicable to the individual context.

€300 bonus for pensioners in Italy: what the new Budget provides and which reliefs already exist for consultations at private, non-contracted centres

TD
Poliambulatorio Delta Team
· 2 November 2026 · 5 min read

Among the measures in Italy's 2027 Budget Law, a €300 healthcare card/voucher for pensioners has been confirmed. Here is what is known today and what remains to be defined.

The Budget was approved by the Council of Ministers on 28 October 2026 and is now before Parliament. The measure has already attracted a great deal of interest among our patients, especially to understand whether and how it can also be used at private healthcare facilities that are not accredited with the Italian National Health Service (SSN), such as our medical centre. Let's look at what is known today and, above all, at the tax reliefs that are already in force and can be used straight away.

Measure not yet final

The information in this article is based on the news available in November 2026. The bonus is not yet law: we will update the article as soon as the final text and the implementing decree are published.

What the measure provides, as of today

The provision, included in the 2027 Budget, provides for a contribution of up to €300 aimed in particular at people over 75 with a pension of up to €28,000 a year, for an estimated 5.5 million people and a total cost to the State of €1.6 billion.

According to early reports, the voucher can be used for healthcare expenses not covered by the National Health Service, in particular medicines and specialist consultations. The proposals that emerged during the parliamentary debate also included dental care, prostheses and dentures, home nursing care and the payment of healthcare co-payments ("ticket"), but the final list of eligible expenses has not yet been published.

What still remains to be defined

It is important to be clear on one point: the text is not yet final law.

  • The parliamentary process: the Budget must complete its passage through the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, where changes and amendments are possible.
  • The implementing rules: they will have to set out in detail the requirements, how to apply and any ISEE (means-test) limits. Options under discussion include a social card, a deduction in the tax return or an application through INPS (the Italian social security institute), on the model of the "psychologist bonus".
  • Use at private centres: it has not yet been confirmed whether the bonus can also be used at private, non-contracted centres like ours. The official sources available so far speak in general of "expenses not covered by the SSN", without specifying whether this includes services provided by non-accredited private facilities.

Our advice is not to bring forward expenses relying on the bonus before the final text and the implementing decree are published. We will continue to update our patients as soon as official and certain information becomes available.

The reliefs that already exist today for those treated at a private, non-contracted centre

While the final rules of the new bonus are awaited, there are already concrete tools that make it possible to recover part of the cost of consultations and tests at private facilities, including those not accredited with the SSN.

19% tax deduction on healthcare expenses

Anyone who pays for specialist consultations, diagnostic tests, laboratory analyses or other healthcare services at a private centre can deduct 19% of the amount exceeding the threshold of €129.11 in their Italian tax return (730 or Modello Redditi). The deduction applies regardless of whether the facility is contracted with the SSN: what matters is that the service is of a healthcare nature and that a proper invoice or equivalent tax document is issued, which should be kept carefully.

100% deductibility for people with disabilities

For people with a recognised disability, certain healthcare and specific assistance expenses are fully deductible from income, not just eligible for the 19% deduction, with a greater tax benefit.

Traceable payments

To benefit from the deductions, payment for private healthcare services must be traceable (debit card, credit card, bank transfer, cheque), except for the exceptions provided by law for certain categories of expense.

Supplementary health funds and private insurance

Many pensioners and workers have, through a former employer, a professional fund or an individual policy, supplementary health cover that reimburses, in whole or in part, services received at private, non-contracted centres too. Check your contract or your pension position: these protections are often under-used simply for lack of information.

Specific bonuses already active

There are measures already in operation, such as the "psychologist bonus", which provides a contribution towards psychotherapy sessions, including with non-accredited private professionals, within the ISEE income limits and according to the procedures published by INPS.

What to do in the meantime

For our patients the practical advice is simple:

  • keep all invoices and receipts for the healthcare services you receive;
  • check your position with regard to any supplementary health funds or policies you already have;
  • wait for the publication of the final text of the 2027 Budget and of the implementing decrees, to understand with certainty whether and how the €300 bonus can also be used for services provided by private, non-contracted facilities.

Our administrative staff are available to help you obtain the necessary tax documentation (invoices, receipts, statements), useful both for the deductions already available today and for any future use of the bonus, as soon as the rules are officially defined.
